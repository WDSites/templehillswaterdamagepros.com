Seasons Of Water Trouble In Clinton

Spring and summer storms are the first source of calls. Heavy rain finds roof flashing, window wells and finished basements, and a slow ceiling stain after a storm often means water has been in the attic insulation for days. Check the ceiling below the roof after any storm that dropped a lot of rain.

Late summer brings tropical weather. Prince George's County lists Hurricane Connie in August 1955, Tropical Storm Lee in September 2011 and other storms in its flood history, and the county describes how low spots can flood even when they are not near a stream. Winter is the season of frozen supply lines and burst pipes. A pipe in an outside wall or a garage freezes, splits and releases water when it thaws, often while nobody is home.

Year-round, appliances cause the quiet losses. A refrigerator ice maker line, a dishwasher hose or an air-conditioner condensate line can leak for weeks before anyone looks. In Clinton's larger houses, that leak may be behind a wall far from the kitchen.

When Water Is Spreading Through A Clinton House

A bigger house means water can travel further before it shows. If you hear running water you cannot trace, or see a stain growing, treat it as an emergency and call. Our dispatch line is answered around the clock.

Find the main

Locate the main shutoff, often near the meter or where the line enters the house, and close it.

Cut power if dry

Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry floor.

Raise valuables

Move electronics, papers and small furniture upward and away from the wet area.

Stay out of dirty water

If water came through a drain or from outside, keep children and pets out.

Photograph

Take photos of the damage and the source before you move anything.

A Community With A Long History And A Wide Footprint

Clinton began in the 1770s as a crossroads called Surratt's Villa and was renamed Clinton in 1879. The Surratt House Museum preserves that history, and the commercial property at the main intersection dates to the 1950s. The community counted 13,725 housing units in the 2020 census, spread over a wide area with a vacancy rate of about 3 percent.

That spread matters in an emergency, because a crew traveling to a lot off a long road needs a clear address and a good description of where the water is.

Tell the dispatcher the road, any gate or code, and whether the water is in a basement, a ground floor or an upper level. Houses of different ages call for different checks. A 1950s house may have cast iron or galvanized lines, while a newer one is more likely to have flexible supply hoses and engineered flooring.

Creeks South Of The Beltway

The county's flood studies include Piscataway Creek, dated April 1986, and Tinkers Creek, dated June 1979. Piscataway Creek begins at Joint Base Andrews, one of Clinton's neighbors, and runs to the Potomac. Whether your lot falls near a mapped stream depends on your address, so check the county's flood map information and ask your insurer.

Flood water is Category 3, which means carpet, pad and lower drywall are removed rather than dried. For storm and basement jobs, see flood damage restoration, and for pumping standing water see emergency water removal.

Industry Standards And Safety Basics

Professional water work in Clinton is commonly guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. It sorts water by contamination category and drying by moisture readings rather than by guesswork. Until a crew arrives, stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings, do not use electrical devices near standing water, and do not mix cleaning products. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911.

We cover ZIP 20735, the ZIP code used by Clinton, from the older crossroads near the main intersection to the newer subdivisions on the outskirts. If your address is in a neighboring ZIP, call and the dispatcher will confirm coverage.

Can a slow roof leak cause mold inside the walls?

Yes. A slow leak keeps insulation and drywall damp, and mold needs moisture. Fix the roof, dry the area and have it assessed. See the mold remediation page for how that works.

What is the best way to describe my location to the dispatcher?

Give the street name, any gate code or long driveway details, and where the water is in the house. If you can, say whether the source is a pipe, an appliance, the roof or outside water.

Does a frozen pipe burst come under water damage restoration?

Yes. The crew extracts water, dries the structure and documents the loss. A plumber repairs the pipe. Insurance guidance says a homeowners policy generally covers damage from a sudden burst but not the pipe itself.
