Temple Hills Water Damage Pros handles water damage, flood cleanup and mold remediation for Temple Hills, Oxon Hill, Clinton and Suitland. Crews are licensed and insured. We do not publish a license number, a technician name or a count of jobs on this site, and we do not show reviews or ratings, because we would rather leave out a claim than overstate it.

How We Work

The dispatch line at (301) 936-0619 is answered around the clock. The dispatcher asks where the water is, where it came from and whether anyone is in danger, then explains what can happen tonight and what can wait. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

On site, the crew assesses before it acts. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where the water went. The scope and price are explained before work starts, so you can decide with the numbers in front of you.

Standards We Follow

Water damage restoration follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520. These are published industry standards. We name them so you can ask any company, including us, whether its crews work to them.

What We Do Not Do

  • We do not promise that an insurance claim will be approved or that an insurer will pay us directly.
  • We do not promise an arrival time.
  • We do not waterproof foundations. If water keeps entering through walls or floors, a drainage or waterproofing contractor addresses the cause.
  • We do not make health claims about mold. Those questions belong with a physician.

Where We Are Based

Our address is 3175 Brinkley Road, Temple Hills, MD 20748. Temple Hills is an unincorporated community in Prince George's County, and the Maryland State Data Center's 2020 Census tables count 3,683 housing units there, with 2,335 renter-occupied and 1,135 owner-occupied. That mix of apartments and houses is why our pages cover both.

The same Census tables list 213 vacant units. A leak in an empty unit can run for weeks before anyone notices, so we explain on our pages how to spot slow leaks and what to do when you are not the one living in the unit.

What A Call Looks Like

A call starts with a few questions. Where is the water, what is the source, is anyone hurt, and can the source be shut off safely? The dispatcher tells you what to do before a crew arrives, such as closing a valve, staying off wet floors and photographing damage for your insurer.

If a crew is available, it is sent the same day. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, weather and other emergencies change it. We would rather tell you that plainly than set an expectation we cannot meet.

Insurance And Documentation

Insurance coverage depends on your policy and the cause of the loss, and your insurer makes that decision, not us. What we can do is document the job. Crews record moisture readings, photograph the damage and keep a list of what was removed, so you have a clear record to hand to an adjuster.

Flood water and plumbing water are often handled under different policies, and FEMA notes that flood insurance can take up to 30 days to become active. For that reason we suggest reading your policy before a storm, not after one.

Working With Landlords And Property Managers

Many Temple Hills households rent, so a lot of calls come from tenants. If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager right away and keep copies of your messages. The lease and the building owner decide who authorizes work, and we explain what we find so everyone can decide quickly. Photos of the damage, the date it started and the name of whoever you notified will all help later.

Mold And Moisture Questions

Mold usually follows water that stayed too long. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours, and that window is why we ask how long things have been wet. If mold is already visible, do not scrub it dry or run a fan over it. Call, describe what you see, and we will explain what a containment and drying plan involves.
