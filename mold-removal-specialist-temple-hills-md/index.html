Choosing A Mold Removal Specialist In Temple Hills, MD

The word specialist covers very different businesses. Know what each one does before you call.

A mold removal specialist in Temple Hills could be an assessor who tests and writes a protocol, a remediation contractor who does the removal or a general handyman who does a bit of both. The titles overlap, and the difference matters because a company that assesses and removes in the same job has a conflict of interest, which is why some people split the work.

Assessment, Remediation And Clearance

RoleWhat it doesWho often does it
AssessmentInspects, measures moisture, may sample, writes a planAn independent assessor or a remediation company
RemediationContains, removes and cleans per a written scopeA remediation contractor
ClearanceChecks that the work is complete, sometimes with samplingAn independent party if you want an unbiased check
RepairRebuilds drywall, flooring and finishesA general contractor or the same company

Maryland's 2008 licensing law, as described in a 2016 state evaluation, defined mold remediation as the removal, cleaning, sanitizing, demolition or other treatment of mold or mold-contaminated matter, and it excluded mold assessments and structural repairs after remediation. That distinction is useful even if you are not looking for a license. Remediation, assessment and rebuilding are different jobs.

Questions To Ask Every Company

  • What will you do about the moisture source, and who fixes it?
  • What is in the written scope, and what is not included?
  • What standard do you follow? The published standard is ANSI/IICRC S520, and a company should be able to explain what it means in practice.
  • Will you contain the area, and how?
  • How will you tell me the work is finished, and will I get documentation?
  • Can I see proof of liability insurance?
  • What happens if the job turns out to be larger than expected?

What A Good Written Scope Contains

A good scope names the rooms, the materials to be removed, the containment method, the cleaning steps, the drying plan, the documentation you will receive and the exclusions. It should tell you whether rebuilding is included. A scope that says only mold removal with a number attached leaves room for argument later.

Red Flags

  • A fixed price by phone without seeing the property.
  • A promise that mold will never return, since mold needs moisture.
  • Pressure to sign the same day or pay everything up front.
  • No mention of the moisture source.
  • Health claims about what mold has done or will do to you.
  • No proof of insurance.

What To Expect On The First Visit

A good first visit is mostly looking and asking. The visitor checks where the moisture is, takes readings, looks at the history of leaks or floods and asks what you want, whether that is a clean closet, a clearance for a sale or a full remediation. You should leave with a clear idea of what the scope will include and when you will receive it in writing.

Mold work is priced by what has to be done, so these are the factors that set the price.

  • Area affected. A small closet and a whole basement are different jobs.
  • Materials. Porous materials are removed, while framing may be cleaned.
  • Containment. Barriers and air filtration add setup and time.
  • Testing. Any sampling or clearance step is a separate item.
  • Rebuild. New drywall, insulation and paint are usually separate.

Insurance may cover mold that results from a covered water loss, but many policies limit mold coverage, so read your policy and ask your agent.

What Maryland Did And Did Not Put In Place

The Maryland Department of Legislative Services reported in 2016 that Chapter 537 of 2008 created a licensing requirement for mold remediation firms under the Maryland Home Improvement Commission, requiring certified workers and at least $1 million in liability insurance. It found the program had never been implemented because no start-up funding was provided, and that a 2011 law had extended the deadline to July 1, 2013, which also passed.

The practical tie-back is that you cannot assume a company is licensed for mold work simply because it advertises. Ask what it is required to hold today, and ask for proof of liability insurance. Rules can change after a 2016 report, so confirm with the state if the answer matters to your decision.

Common Questions

Should the company that finds the mold also remove it?

It is common, but some buyers prefer an independent assessor and independent clearance so the person checking is not the person being paid to remove. Ask what you want from the process and decide.

What is ANSI/IICRC S520?

It is the published industry standard for professional mold remediation. A company can say its crews work to it, and you can ask what that means in their scope. We name it as a standard and do not claim a certification.

Is mold testing required before removal?

Not always. The EPA says that if visible mold growth is present, sampling is generally unnecessary. Testing can be useful for specific purposes, such as clearance after the work.

Does a mold company in Temple Hills need a Maryland license?

A 2008 Maryland law created a licensing requirement, and a 2016 state evaluation found it had not been put into effect. Rules can change, so ask any company what it is required to hold today and ask for proof of liability insurance.
