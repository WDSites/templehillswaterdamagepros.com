Burst Pipe Water Damage In Temple Hills, MD

When a pipe lets go, minutes count. This guide covers the shutoff, the first hour and the cleanup that follows.

Burst pipe water damage in Temple Hills tends to arrive in two forms. One is the sudden break, a supply line or a fitting that fails and sends water into a wall or a floor. The other is the freeze split, where a pipe in a cold wall or garage cracks in winter and releases water when it thaws. Both are emergencies, and both are limited by how fast you can stop the flow.

The First Ten Minutes

  1. Find the main water shutoff and close it. It is often near the water meter or where the line enters the house. In an apartment, ask the manager where the unit or riser shutoff is.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain the line and relieve pressure.
  3. If water is near outlets or appliances, stay out of the room. Turn power off at the panel only if you can reach it on dry floor.
  4. Move valuables, electronics and papers out of the wet area.
  5. Take photos and video of the source and the damage.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water is and where it came from.

Electrical risk comes before the cleanup

A pipe that bursts above a ceiling can send water through light fixtures. If you see water in a fixture or hear buzzing, stay out, cut power only from a safe, dry position and call 911 if you see sparks or smell burning.

Why A Burst Pipe Does So Much Damage

A broken supply line is under pressure, so the flow is far larger than a drip. Water follows the framing, runs down wall cavities and collects in the floor below. By the time the water is shut off, the cavity may be soaked from top to bottom.

That is why crews open walls near the break and check insulation.

Insulation that is wet stays wet, and it keeps moisture against the studs and drywall. Quick extraction and drying are the difference between a drywall patch and a rebuilt room.

Frozen Pipe Splits In Winter

A pipe that freezes can split without leaking until it thaws, which is why the damage often appears on a warmer afternoon.

If you find a frozen pipe, do not heat it with an open flame. Open the faucet, warm the area gently with a hair dryer or by raising the room temperature, and keep the main valve within reach in case the pipe has already split.

Check the National Weather Service forecast for hard freezes and let faucets on exterior walls drip on those nights.

Who Fixes What

A plumber repairs the pipe. A restoration crew extracts the water, dries the structure and documents the loss, as described under water mitigation. Insurance guidance from the Maryland Insurance Administration says a homeowners policy generally covers water damage from a pipe that suddenly bursts but not the cost of repairing or replacing the pipe. Photograph the break before the plumber replaces it.

What To Record For The Plumber And The Insurer

  • Photos of the broken pipe or fitting before it is replaced, with the date.
  • Photos and video of every wet room, ceiling, wall and floor.
  • The time you found the leak and the time the water was shut off.
  • A list of damaged belongings, with age and approximate value.
  • The plumber's invoice and a note of what failed, since insurers often ask.

Common Questions

How much water can a burst pipe release?

It depends on the pipe size and pressure, but a broken supply line can release a large amount of water in an hour. That is why shutting the main valve quickly is the most valuable step.

Does insurance cover a burst pipe in a Temple Hills home?

A homeowners policy generally covers water damage from a pipe that suddenly bursts but not the cost of repairing the pipe itself, according to the Maryland Insurance Administration. Check your policy, and ask your insurer about freeze exclusions if the house was unheated.

Who do I call if the leak is in the street and not in my house?

Call WSSC Water. It lists 301-206-4001 for customer service and 301-206-4002 for emergencies. Call us for water inside the house.

Should I leave the heat on when I travel to avoid frozen pipes?

Yes. Keep the house heated, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and consider asking someone to check the house during a long cold stretch. Many policies have conditions about maintaining heat, so check yours.
