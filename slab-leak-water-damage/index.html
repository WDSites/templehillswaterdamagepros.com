A slab leak is a leak in a water line under or in a concrete floor. How common it is varies by house. Basements and crawl spaces are different foundations, and we say plainly that slab leaks are one of several causes of wet floors here, not the most common one. If you suspect one, call (301) 936-0619. The line is answered 24/7.

What A Slab Leak Is, And Is Not

Many slab-on-grade homes route supply lines through or under the concrete. When a line corrodes or shifts, water escapes into the gravel and soil under the floor, and it may rise through cracks or at the edges. It is not a sewer problem, and it is not the same as water seeping through a basement wall.

Because the pipe is hidden, you rarely see the break. You see its effects: a floor that feels warm, a water bill that jumps, the sound of running water when everything is off, or damp flooring along a wall.

Clues That Point Below The Floor

Meter keeps moving

Shut off every fixture and watch the meter. If it still turns, water is escaping somewhere.

Warm patch on the floor

A hot-water line leaking under a slab can warm the concrete above it.

Damp carpet at one spot

Moisture rising from below shows up first where the break is nearest.

Sound without a source

A hiss or trickle under the floor with all taps off.

Safety First When Water Rises Through Floors

Slab leak safety

Shut off the main water valve and keep water-using appliances off. Stay off wet floors near outlets and panels, and turn off power to a wet area if you can do so from a dry spot. Do not break the slab yourself.

How We Dry A Slab Leak Loss

The crew uses moisture meters and a thermal camera to map the wet area, then extracts surface water. Carpet and pad are lifted if saturated, and baseboard comes off so walls can be checked.

Concrete holds moisture, so drying can take longer than on a wood floor. Dehumidifiers and air movers run until concrete and framing readings are back to normal, using the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as a reference. Flooring that was glued to the slab may need to be removed.

Wider drying is described under structural drying and dehumidification, and floor-level pooling is covered by emergency water removal.

Slab leak cleanup varies mostly with how far the water spread and how long it ran.

  • Duration. Weeks of leaking soak more than a quick discovery.
  • Flooring. Glued flooring and tile are harder to dry than carpet.
  • Concrete drying. Slab moisture takes more days to read normal.
  • Rooms involved. More rooms mean more equipment.
  • Plumber timing. Drying is delayed if the leak is still active.

Slab jobs vary widely, so call (301) 936-0619 to hear scope and price before drying equipment is set.

Insurance And Slab Leaks

Many policies treat sudden discharge differently from gradual leakage, and a slab leak can fall on either side depending on how long it went on. Some policies also treat the cost of reaching a buried pipe separately. Keep the plumber's report, and we will give you photos, readings and a removal list. Your insurer decides coverage.
