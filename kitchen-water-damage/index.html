Water in a kitchen touches many materials at once. Cabinet boxes, toe-kicks, countertops, backsplash, subfloor and the floor surface each hold water differently. If your kitchen is wet right now, call (301) 936-0619. The line is answered 24/7.

Where Kitchen Water Comes From

The usual sources are the sink supply and drain, the dishwasher connection, a refrigerator water line and a leaking faucet or disposal. Slow leaks under the sink are common because the cabinet floor hides them. Larger events come from a failed supply hose or a pot filler line.

Because the sink base cabinet is a closed box, water collects there and wicks into the particleboard sides. A cabinet that looks dry at the door may be soaked at the back and bottom.

What Each Layer Tells You

Cabinet bases

Swelling at the bottom edge or a loose toe-kick means water reached the particleboard.

Countertop and backsplash

Lifting laminate or stained caulk points to water from above or behind.

Flooring

Cupping, buckling or lifting seams show water below the surface.

Walls

Paint bubbles or soft drywall behind appliances signal moisture in the cavity.

First-Hour Kitchen Safety

Kitchen water safety

Close the supply valve under the sink or the appliance valve, or the main if you cannot find them. Keep power off to outlets near the wet floor and do not use the oven or dishwasher until an electrician or a qualified technician checks them.

Empty the sink base and the wet cabinet and set aside food and dishes that touched water. Take photos of every layer before you wipe anything.

How We Dry A Wet Kitchen

Extraction comes first. The crew removes toe-kicks and checks cabinet bases, drilling small drying holes in a base only when it is the practical option. Wet insulation and drywall behind cabinets are cut out where needed.

Dehumidifiers and air movers run with directed airflow into cabinet bases, and drying mats can pull moisture from a hardwood or engineered floor. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and we plan the work around that window. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides our drying steps as a reference.

A wet kitchen can mean a damp toe-kick or a full gut. The cost depends on these factors.

  • Cabinet material. Particleboard swells and often needs replacement, while solid wood dries better.
  • Flooring type. Tile, vinyl, laminate and hardwood differ in what can be saved.
  • Water duration. A long slow leak reaches farther than a one-time spill.
  • Countertops. Removing countertops to dry cabinets adds work.
  • Rebuild scope. Cabinet and flooring replacement is separate from drying.

Insurance Fit For A Kitchen Loss

Sudden leaks from plumbing or appliances are often treated differently from long-term seepage, and the policy wording decides. We provide photos, readings and a list of materials removed. Your insurer decides coverage, and a flooring or cabinet contractor handles the rebuild estimate.

Prevention And Mistakes To Avoid

  • Look under the sink once a month with a flashlight.
  • Replace aging supply hoses before they fail.
  • Do not seal a wet cabinet base with caulk or paint.
  • Do not install new flooring over a subfloor that has not been dried.

Common Questions

Is emergency kitchen water damage service available in Temple Hills?

The line never closes, and a crew can come to a wet kitchen the same day when one is available. Close the supply valve and keep power off near the wet floor while you wait.

Can wet kitchen cabinets be saved?

Solid wood often can, with prompt drying. Particleboard that has swelled usually cannot be restored. We check each cabinet before recommending removal.

Do you replace countertops and flooring?

We handle extraction, drying and removal of unsalvageable materials. A flooring or cabinet contractor handles the rebuild, and our scope notes help them price it.

Why check behind the dishwasher?

Dishwasher leaks often run along the subfloor and into the cabinet base, so the surface may look dry while materials behind and below are wet.
