A Wide Community With A Homeowner Majority

Camp Springs is an unincorporated community and census-designated place covering roughly 7.7 square miles of Prince George's County. The county's Census 2020 synopsis lists 22,734 residents, and the Maryland State Data Center counts 9,114 housing units, of which 506 were vacant.

Of the 8,608 occupied homes, 5,814 are owner-occupied and 2,794 are rented, so about 68 percent are owner households. When a pipe fails in an owner household, the homeowner decides on the repair, calls the insurer and picks the restoration crew.

From A Crossroads To A 1960s And 1970s Growth Spurt

Local history summaries say the community grew from a crossroads at Branch Avenue and Allentown Road and that its largest building boom came in the 1960s and 1970s, after water and sewer lines were installed and the Capital Beltway was built. Joint Base Andrews sits next to the community.

Housing from that boom is now about 50 to 60 years old. Age alone does not mean a house will leak, but appliances, water heaters and supply hoses wear out on their own schedule, and it is worth knowing how old yours are.

Safety Steps For A Leaking House

Camp Springs safety steps

Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely, and stay out of rooms where water reaches outlets. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water. If a ceiling is bulging, keep clear of it.

Move belongings that are easy to carry to a dry room, and photograph everything before moving it. If the water came from a sewer line or outdoors, keep children and pets out until crews have cleaned the area.

Insurance Documentation For Homeowners

Your policy and the cause decide coverage. Sudden accidental damage is often treated differently from gradual leaks, and flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy. We record moisture readings, take photographs and list removed materials so you can give your insurer a clear file.

Industry Standards And Safety Basics
