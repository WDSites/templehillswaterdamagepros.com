My Ceiling Is Dripping And The Carpet Is Soaked. What Now?

That scene has a trade name, and it is water mitigation. Mitigation means limiting the damage that has already happened and preventing the damage that is about to happen. It is different from the rebuild, which comes later and is covered on the water damage repair page.

A soaked carpet over a damp pad over a wet subfloor does not dry by itself in a closed house. Moisture climbs into drywall by wicking, and it spreads into insulation and framing where you cannot see it. Mitigation crews work to stop that spread before mold starts, and the mold remediation page explains what happens if it is missed.

What Water Mitigation Includes

1

Source control

The crew finds the leak or intrusion point and confirms the water is off, so drying does not fight a live source.

2

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show which walls, floors and ceilings are wet, including areas that look dry.

3

Extraction

Pumps, wet vacuums and extraction units remove standing and soaked-in water before drying starts.

4

Drying setup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed, and readings are logged each day until the structure reaches its drying goal.

When Standing Water Is An Emergency

Any water above the floor level of a finished room is an emergency, and so is any water in a wall cavity that has been wet more than a day. Water that reaches an outlet, a baseboard heater or the area around a panel changes the job from a drying problem to a safety problem. If you see that, stay out and call us before you touch anything.

If you want standing water pumped out right away, the emergency water removal page covers extraction in detail. Most mitigation calls involve both, because water has to come out before it can be dried.

Electrical and gas risk comes first

If water is touching electrical panels, outlets or appliances, do not enter the room. Turn off power at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the building and call 911 and your utility before you call us.

Structural Drying: What The Equipment Does

Structural drying is the part of mitigation that takes longest. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, which evaporates moisture into the room. LGR dehumidifiers then pull that moisture out of the air so it is not simply reabsorbed by the walls. Opening wall cavities with small holes or removing baseboards lets air reach the studs and insulation.

Equipment is sized to the room and checked every day. A moisture meter reading on a stud or a subfloor tells the crew whether the structure is approaching the dry standard for that material. Equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not on a fixed calendar. Wet hardwood, plaster and thick insulation usually take longer than painted drywall.

Industry Standards And Safety Basics

Professional water work in Temple Hills is commonly guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. It sorts water by contamination category and drying by moisture readings rather than by guesswork. Until a crew arrives, stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings, do not use electrical devices near standing water, and do not mix cleaning products. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911.

Common Questions

Do you handle water mitigation emergencies at night or on weekends in Temple Hills?

Yes. The line at (301) 936-0619 is answered 24/7. After-hours calls get the same intake as daytime calls, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

What is the difference between water mitigation and water damage repair?

Mitigation stops and dries. Repair rebuilds. Mitigation comes first and usually includes extraction, moisture mapping and drying equipment, while repair covers drywall, insulation, flooring and paint once the structure is dry.

Can I dry it myself with box fans and a shop vac?

A shop vac and fans can help with a small spill on a hard floor. They cannot dry wall cavities, subfloors or padding, and they cannot measure whether the framing is dry. If water has been inside the walls or under flooring for more than a day, a moisture meter reading is the only way to know the job is done.

How do I know my house is actually dry?

Ask for the daily moisture readings. The standard for a restoration company is documented readings that reach the drying goal for each material, not a room that merely feels dry.
