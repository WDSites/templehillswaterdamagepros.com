Appliance leaks are quiet. A washer hose weeps behind the machine, a dishwasher seal drips into the cabinet base or an ice-maker line loosens behind a refrigerator, and the damage builds out of sight. If water is already on the floor, call (301) 936-0619. The line is answered 24/7.

Which Appliances Cause The Damage

Washing machines are a frequent source because they connect to both a supply and a drain. The rubber hoses age, the clamps loosen and the drain standpipe can overflow. Dishwashers leak at the door gasket, the supply fitting under the sink or the drain hose. Refrigerator leaks usually trace to the water line feeding an ice maker or dispenser.

Others include window air conditioners, dehumidifiers and portable humidifiers, and a drain pan that overflows. Each releases a different amount of water, but the pattern is the same: it travels along the subfloor and wets cabinet bases, baseboard and the ceiling below.

Why Appliance Leaks Matter In Apartments

The Maryland State Data Center's 2020 Census tables show 2,335 of the 3,470 occupied housing units in the Temple Hills census-designated place are renter-occupied. In stacked units, a washer overflow in one apartment can reach the ceiling of the unit below, which turns a personal problem into a neighbor problem.

Prince George's County's DPIE handles property code enforcement, and tenants who cannot get a landlord to respond can ask DPIE about their options. Report in writing, keep photos and call us for the water itself.

What To Do Before We Arrive

Appliance leak safety

Shut off the water valve to the appliance, and unplug it only if the cord and outlet are dry. If the outlet is wet or you are standing in water, shut off the breaker for that circuit instead, and do not touch the plug.

Move rugs, boxes and furniture off the wet area. Take photos of the appliance, the floor and any ceilings below before cleaning up.

How We Handle The Water

The crew extracts the water, then maps the wet area with moisture meters rather than trusting the surface. The appliance is pulled out if possible so the wall and floor behind it can be checked.

Toe-kicks and baseboard come off, and small holes may be drilled to dry a wall cavity. Air movers and a dehumidifier then run until the readings fall, following the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as a reference. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why we move fast on a soaked pad or cabinet base.

Appliance leaks look small and price out in many ways. The job follows these factors.

  • Leak duration. Days of dripping soak more than one overflow.
  • Subfloor type. Slab, plywood and tile over wood respond differently.
  • Number of floors. A leak that reached a ceiling below adds a second work area.
  • Cabinets and walls. Wet cabinet bases and drywall add removal.
  • Equipment days. Cabinet cavities dry more slowly than open floors.

Insurance Questions After An Appliance Leak

Sudden discharge from an appliance is often treated differently from a slow drip that went on for weeks, and the policy wording controls. Some policies cover water damage but not the appliance. We photograph and document the loss, and your insurer decides coverage. Renters should check whether the building policy or a personal renter's policy applies.

Prevention And Mistakes To Avoid

  • Replace old rubber supply hoses with braided ones before they fail.
  • Do not push a washer tight against the wall, which can kink the hose.
  • Do not wipe a wet toe-kick dry and assume the cabinet base is fine.
  • Do not run a fan across a wet floor and stop there, because wet pad and subfloor stay wet.

Common Questions

Is emergency appliance leak water damage service available in Temple Hills?

Yes. A dispatcher picks up at any hour, and a washer or dishwasher flood gets a crew the same day when one is free. Shut off the appliance's water valve and photograph the leak before you move anything.

Should I unplug a leaking washer or dishwasher?

Only if the cord and outlet are dry and you are not standing in water. Otherwise shut off the breaker for that circuit and leave the plug alone.

The floor looks dry. Is there still damage?

Possibly. Water runs under cabinet bases and into subfloors, so we use moisture meters to check beyond what you can see.

My neighbor's appliance flooded my apartment. What should I do?

Report it in writing to your landlord, document the damage with photos and call us for water removal. DPIE, the county's property code enforcement office, can advise if a landlord does not respond.
