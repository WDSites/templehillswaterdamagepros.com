Safety Steps For A Commercial Loss

Commercial water safety

Close the main shut-off and keep occupants out of rooms with sagging ceilings or standing water near electrical equipment. Switch off power to wet areas only from a dry position, and do not use computers, copiers or appliances that touched water.

Take photographs and notes of what is wet, move inventory and records to dry areas if it is safe and notify the property manager and insurer.

How Commercial Drying Works

The crew extracts standing water with truck-mounted or portable extractors as the space allows. Moisture meters and thermal imaging then map wet ceilings, walls and floors, including areas above suspended ceilings.

Wet carpet, pad and ceiling tiles are removed when they cannot be dried, and air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings return to normal. Drying plans follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard as a reference. For equipment and process detail, see structural drying and dehumidification.

Insurance And Property Responsibility

Commercial claims often involve both the property owner's policy and a tenant's policy, and lease terms decide who pays for what. We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and a list of removed material, and the insurers decide coverage. Keep your lease and policy handy when you call.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Waiting for an adjuster before starting extraction and drying.
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles in place above equipment.
  • Restarting HVAC while ducts and ceilings are wet.
  • Skipping written notice to the property owner or manager.
