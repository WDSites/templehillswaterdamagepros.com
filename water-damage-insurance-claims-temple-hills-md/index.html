Water Damage Insurance Claims In Temple Hills, MD

Policies differ, and the cause of the water matters more than the amount. This is a plain guide to the usual rules and how to document a loss.

Water damage insurance in Temple Hills comes down to one question: where did the water come from? A policy that pays for a burst pipe may pay nothing for a flooded basement, and a gradual leak behind a wall may be treated differently from a sudden break. This guide explains the general rules, using guidance from the Maryland Insurance Administration, and it does not replace your policy.

Sudden Damage Versus Gradual Damage

Homeowners policies generally cover damage from a sudden and accidental event, such as a pipe that bursts or an appliance that fails all at once. They tend not to cover damage from something that happened slowly over time, such as a leak under a sink that was ignored for months. The wording of your policy decides, so read the section on water damage and the list of exclusions.

The Maryland Insurance Administration says a homeowners policy generally covers water damage from a pipe that suddenly bursts, but not the cost of repairing or replacing the pipe itself.

The restoration of floors, drywall and belongings may be covered, while the plumber's bill for the pipe is often yours.

Flood Is Different From Water Damage

According to the Maryland Insurance Administration, homeowners policies generally do not cover flooding, and flood coverage comes from a separate policy such as one through the National Flood Insurance Program. The program's definition of a flood is a general and temporary inundation of two or more acres of normally dry land or of two or more properties, one of which is yours.

Seepage through a foundation wall or floor is not covered by a flood policy, and homeowners policies generally treat it as a maintenance problem. That is one reason a basement that gets damp every spring is a coverage question to ask about before a loss.

The Backup Endorsement

Backup damage from a sump pump overflow or a sewer line that backs up through drains is not covered by many standard policies unless you add a water and sewer backup endorsement.

The Maryland Insurance Administration says Maryland law requires insurers to offer this coverage, and most offer several limits. It usually pays for damage from the backed-up water, not for fixing the drain itself. Ask your agent whether you have it, because it is far cheaper to add before a loss than to wish for it after.

How To Document A Water Loss

  1. Stop the water and take photos and video of the source and the damage before you move things.
  2. Write down what was damaged, with age and approximate value.
  3. Keep receipts for emergency steps and drying, and ask for written scopes.
  4. Save moisture readings and drying logs from the restoration company.
  5. Notify your insurer promptly and note the date, the name of the person you spoke to and what was said.
  6. Do not throw away damaged items until the adjuster has seen them or approved their disposal, unless they are a health hazard.

Flood Insurance And The County Flood Map

Prince George's County states that all waterways are subject to flooding, and that low spots can flood even when they are not near a stream. The county also says its first countywide flood insurance rate map is dated August 4, 1972, with the most recent revised panels dated September 6, 1996 and a preliminary countywide map published by FEMA on September 16, 2016.

The practical tie-back is that a map date does not tell you your own risk, so look up your address and ask your lender or agent whether a flood zone applies.

FEMA says flood insurance can take up to 30 days to become active, so buying after a storm warning is too late.

Common Questions

Does homeowners insurance pay for water damage in Maryland?

Often, if the water damage was sudden and accidental. It generally does not pay for flooding, seepage or neglect. The Maryland Insurance Administration recommends reading your policy and talking with your insurance professional.

Do I need a water and sewer backup endorsement in Temple Hills?

That depends on your risk and budget, but it is the coverage that pays for damage from a sump pump overflow or sewer backup. Maryland law requires insurers to offer it. Ask your agent for the limit options and the cost.

Will the restoration company deal directly with my insurer?

We document the loss and provide a scope you can send to your adjuster. We do not promise the claim will be approved and we do not bill insurers directly. You work with your insurer on coverage.

I live near Henson Creek. Do I need flood insurance?

The county has a flood study for the Henson Creek watershed from April 1986 and says low spots can flood anywhere. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your lender or agent whether a flood policy applies. A waiting period can apply before a new policy takes effect.
