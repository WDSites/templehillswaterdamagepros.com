Water Damage Restoration For Temple Hills Homes And Apartments

Water damage restoration is the work of finding where water came from, removing what is standing, drying everything it touched and rebuilding what cannot be saved. In Temple Hills, most of that work happens in homes built between the 1950s and the 1970s, a mix of garden apartments, duplexes and single-family houses with the plumbing, roofs and basements that came with them.

Do This First

If Water Is Spreading, Do These Things Before We Arrive

These steps are about limiting the loss and keeping people safe. They do not replace a professional assessment, and none of them should put you at risk.

1

Stop the source

Shut the fixture valve or the main water valve. Know where the main valve is before you need it.

2

Mind the power

If water touches outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out. Cut power only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry floor.

3

Lift what you can

Raise furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and move rugs, electronics and papers to a dry room.

4

Call the line

Call (301) 936-0619, describe what happened and take photos of the damage before you move things.

Sewage, gas smell or sparking outlets

If the water looks or smells like sewage, keep children and pets out and do not wade through it. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the building and call 911 and your utility before calling us.

For a sewer emergency on the public side, WSSC Water lists 301-206-4002 on its utility services page. That is the utility's emergency line for sewer problems, and it is separate from our dispatch line.

Local Causes

Why Water Damage Happens In Temple Hills

Temple Hills is an unincorporated community in Prince George's County, about 269 feet above sea level, with most of its housing built from the 1950s through the 1970s. Homes of that age often still have original galvanized or early copper supply lines, original water heaters long since replaced once or twice, and flat or low-slope roof sections over additions. Each is a common starting point for a slow leak that becomes a ceiling stain.

The community takes its name from Edward Temple, who lived beside Henson Creek at a house called Moor Park in the 1860s. Henson Creek is still part of the picture. The county keeps a flood study on file for the Henson Creek watershed dated April 1986.

The county says low spots can flood even when they are nowhere near a stream. If you live on a street that runs downhill toward the creek, ask your insurer and check the FEMA flood map before you assume a basement is safe.

Closer to the Brinkley Road corridor, where our office sits near the Rosecroft Shopping Center, the housing is a mix of garden apartments and duplexes.

In a multi-unit building, a leak upstairs becomes your ceiling, so call early and tell the property manager at the same time. Larger buildings in the neighborhood, from apartment blocks to the Temple Hills Swim Club on its pool plant, carry bigger pipe runs and need a commercial-style assessment of how far water traveled before anyone sets equipment.

Which Kind Of Water Is It?

Water is sorted into three categories, and the category changes what is saved and what is thrown away. This table is a plain-language version of the sorting used in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means
Category 1Supply line, broken pipe, rainwater from a clean sourceClean at first. Materials can often be dried in place if crews start quickly.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without fecesContains contaminants. Porous materials such as carpet pad are usually removed.
Category 3Sewage, creek or river flood water, long-standing waterGrossly contaminated. Porous materials come out and surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

Clean water that sits for more than a day or two can slide into the next category, which is one more reason the first call matters.

Cost And Insurance

What The Price Of A Water Damage Job Depends On

We do not quote a flat price over the phone, because the same leak can cost very different amounts depending on these factors.

  • Water category. Clean, gray or black water changes labor, disposal and safety steps.
  • Affected area. A single bathroom is a different job from a whole floor with wet wall cavities.
  • Materials involved. Hardwood, carpet, drywall, plaster and insulation dry or fail in different ways.
  • Drying days and equipment. More air movers and dehumidifiers over more days cost more than a one-room setup.
  • Rebuild scope. Drywall, flooring and paint come after drying and are priced separately from mitigation.

Want a real number for your property? Call (301) 936-0619 and we will arrange an assessment. The scope and price are explained before work starts.

Insurance questions, answered in general terms

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. The Maryland Insurance Administration explains that homeowners policies generally do not cover flooding and that water and sewer backup coverage is an endorsement you have to carry. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Common Questions

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Temple Hills?

Yes. The dispatch line at (301) 936-0619 is answered around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and the person on the phone will tell you plainly what can happen tonight and what can wait until morning.

How is the price set, and will I know it before work starts?

Pricing depends on the water category, the square footage affected, the materials involved, the number of drying days and how much rebuild is needed. After an assessment of the property, the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Anyone quoting a flat number over the phone without seeing the structure is guessing. Ask what the number includes and what would change it.

Can you work with my homeowners insurance company?

We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and a written scope so you have what an adjuster usually asks for. Whether the loss is covered is decided by your policy and your insurer, so read your policy or call your agent. We do not promise a claim will be approved and we do not bill insurers directly.

My basement keeps taking on water. Can you fix that?

Restoration is not waterproofing. We remove the water, dry the structure and rebuild what was damaged. If water keeps coming back through a foundation wall or floor, the cause is drainage, grading or a failed sump system, and that is work for a waterproofing or drainage contractor. We will tell you honestly which side of that line your problem sits on.

How long does drying take?

Structural drying usually takes several days once air movers and dehumidifiers are set, and wet hardwood, plaster, insulation and wall cavities can take longer. Crews take moisture readings each day and stop equipment only when the readings reach the dry standard for your materials.
