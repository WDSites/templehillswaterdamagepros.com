The First 48 Hours Of Cleanup

Cleanup is the second stage of a water loss. The water has been stopped and the worst of it extracted, and the question becomes what in the room can stay. That decision depends on two things, how long the material was wet and what kind of water wet it. The water mitigation page covers the stopping and drying. This page covers what gets removed, cleaned and treated afterward.

Carpet pad is the usual first casualty, because foam and fiber hold water and contaminants and cost little to replace. Carpet itself can sometimes be cleaned and dried if the water was clean and the response was fast. Drywall that has wicked water above the baseboard, insulation that is saturated, and particleboard cabinet bases tend to be removed. Solid wood and tile often survive.

Gray And Black Water: Cleanup With Safety Steps

Cleanup after a washing machine overflow, toilet overflow or sewer backup is different from cleanup after a clean supply line leak. Those are Category 2 and Category 3 events in the ANSI/IICRC S500 sorting, and they carry bacteria. Crews use gloves, boots and respirators, contain the area with plastic, and remove porous materials rather than trying to rescue them.

Do not scrub sewage with a household mop and bucket, and do not run a regular vacuum over it. If the water came from the public sewer main, WSSC Water lists 301-206-4002 for sewer emergencies on its utility services page. If the backup is inside your house line, call us.

Before you start cleaning anything yourself

Do not touch wet electrical devices, and do not enter a room where water has reached outlets. If anything smells like sewage, treat the water as contaminated and keep everyone out of it. Gas smell or sparks means leaving the building and calling 911 and the utility first.

What You Can Do Before The Crew Arrives

  • Take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved, and write down what was in the room.
  • Lift small items, papers and electronics to a dry area, and leave heavy furniture where it is if moving it is a strain.
  • Open drawers and cabinet doors if the surfaces are dry enough to air, but leave sealed wet cabinets alone.
  • Keep a list of any damaged items with age and rough value, because your insurer may ask.

Industry Standards And Safety Basics

Professional water work in Temple Hills is commonly guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. It sorts water by contamination category and drying by moisture readings rather than by guesswork. Until a crew arrives, stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings, do not use electrical devices near standing water, and do not mix cleaning products. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911.

Common Questions

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Temple Hills?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7 and a crew can be sent the same day when one is available. If the water is dangerous, the dispatcher will tell you what to do while you wait.

How soon after a leak should cleanup start?

As soon as the water is stopped. Wet porous materials begin to smell and grow bacteria within a day or two, and delay can move clean water toward a worse category.

Can carpet be saved after a leak?

Sometimes. Carpet that was wet with clean water and treated quickly can often be cleaned and dried. The pad underneath is usually discarded either way.

Does cleanup include the repairs?

No. Cleanup removes and treats. Rebuilding drywall, flooring and paint comes later and is priced separately.
