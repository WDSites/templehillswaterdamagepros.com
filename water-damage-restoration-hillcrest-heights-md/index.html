A Large Community With A Split Housing Mix

Hillcrest Heights is an unincorporated community and census-designated place in Prince George's County. The county's Census 2020 synopsis lists 15,793 residents, and the Maryland State Data Center counts 7,182 housing units, of which 447 were vacant.

Of the 6,735 occupied units, 3,369 are owner-occupied and 3,366 are rented, an almost even split. That means two kinds of calls arrive from the same streets. Homeowners deal with their own plumbing and insurer, while renters wait on a property manager.

What The Split Means For Water Damage

In a house, you can shut the main valve yourself, call us and begin drying. In a rental, the owner controls the riser, the water heater and often the contractor, so a clear written record helps everyone move quickly.

Photograph the leak source, the wet ceiling and any damaged belongings, and note the time you reported the problem. That record supports a request to the owner or a claim to your insurer.

When The Water Is Already Inside: Safety First

Hillcrest Heights safety steps

Stay out of any room where water is near outlets, and do not stand in water while touching breakers. If a ceiling is sagging with water, keep clear of it. Shut off the water supply only when it is safe and reachable.

If the water came from a sewer line, keep children and pets away and avoid cleaning it yourself, since contaminated water needs professional handling. For gas smell or sparks, leave and call 911.
