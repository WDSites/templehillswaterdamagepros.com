A Community Where Most Households Rent

The 2020 Census count from the Maryland State Data Center shows 2,577 housing units in Marlow Heights, with 2,435 occupied and 142 vacant. Of the occupied units, 1,050 are owner-occupied and 1,385 are rented, which makes roughly 57 percent of households renters.

That mix shapes how water damage plays out here. A leak in one apartment becomes a ceiling stain in the unit below, and a tenant often cannot shut the building riser or authorize repairs alone. The first call that matters is to the property manager, and the second is to us.

The 142 vacant units matter too. A burst supply line in an empty unit can run until a neighbor notices water or a smell, so tell management early if you see a stain from a unit that seems unoccupied.

Safety Steps When Water Is Moving In A Shared Building

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve. If the valve is out of reach, ask the manager to shut the building riser.

Check below

Knock on the door beneath you, since water may already be coming through their ceiling.

Electricity

Do not touch outlets or switches on a wet wall. Use the breaker only from a dry spot.

Lift belongings

Raise furniture onto blocks and carry electronics to a dry room.

Contaminated water

If water came from a toilet or drain, keep children and pets away and do not clean it yourself.

Document

Photograph the source and each wet room before anything is moved.
